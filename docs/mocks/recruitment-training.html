Phase 7 · Recruitment & training (#24) · for your review

Recruitment and training: six decisions

Already built: the invite wizard with the licence, editing and removing a member, and handing over leads and listings when someone leaves. Missing, per the ROADMAP: finding people before they are invited, training them once they are in, and putting them in an office.

1 · Candidates are not contacts

Suggested — strongly

A new candidate record with its own stages: Talking → Interview → Offer → Accepted (or Not going ahead). Contacts are customers: every agent can search them, they get marketing, and the assistant reads them. A person applying for a job must not show up in an agent’s contact search or a bulk email. Candidates are seen only by owners, admins and anyone given the new permission Recruit.

Alternative: a “candidate” tag on contacts — less to build, but a colleague-to-be’s CV would sit beside the buyers, and every contact rule would need an exception.

2 · The AI never scores, ranks or rejects a person

Suggested — strongly

The AI may summarise a CV (each line shown beside the CV words it came from, as in 4.5) and draft emails — invitations to interview, a polite “not this time” — always on an approval card. It gives no score, no “best fit”, no order. Who is hired is a person’s decision, written down by a person. A model that ranks people can carry bias the agency would answer for under discrimination law.

3 · Kept only as long as needed

Suggested

Someone not hired is deleted, with their CV, after a period the agency sets (12 months suggested). A card asks first, a month before, so a promising “not yet” can be kept with a reason. The Australian Privacy Principles expect personal information to be destroyed once it is no longer needed.

4 · Accepted → the invite wizard you already have, plus Office & team and Training

Suggested

“Invite to the team” opens the existing wizard filled in from the candidate (name, email, licence). Two steps are new: Office & team (an office is required when the agency has more than one), and a training path to start on day one. Moving an existing member to another office is the same step from their Team page; their listings keep their office unless the owner ticks them over.

5 · Training is the agency’s own knowledge, in order, with sign-offs

Suggested

A training path is a list the owner builds: read a knowledge-base document (ticked when opened and marked read), do something real (shadow an appraisal, run an open home) signed off by a mentor, or ask the assistant — which already answers from the knowledge base with page references. No quizzes and no AI-written lessons in this step: the agency’s own documents are the source.

Later, if wanted: AI-written questions from a document, kept as drafts until the owner approves them.

6 · Licences and CPD: reminders, not judgments

Suggested

The licence expiry already stored gets reminders to the agent and the owner (60 and 14 days before). Training done elsewhere — a course, a seminar — is logged with its hours by the agent; the hours are added up by SQL against the yearly number the agency enters for its state. A licence is ticked “checked on the state’s public register” by a person, with the date — the platform does not read the register. Recruitment is a skill, off until switched on; Training is available to every agency.

Not in this step

Later
  • Posting job ads to SEEK or LinkedIn; an applicant form on the public site.
  • Employment contracts and e-signing; pay and payroll; police or reference checks.
  • AI-written quizzes or lessons; video courses.
