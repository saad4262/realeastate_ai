Phase 7 · Team workload board (#12) · for your review

Team tasks and workload: six decisions

Built already: team messages and meetings, assigning leads and contacts, handing everything over when someone leaves, reminders, a contact’s “next action”, and assistant plans that run by themselves. Missing: a to-do a person owns, shares and ticks off — and a view of who is carrying what.

1 · One task list; a contact’s “next action” becomes a task

Suggested

A new task: what, who does it, when it is due, optionally linked to a contact, lead, listing, deal or tenancy, and who or what made it (a person, a workflow, an approved assistant card). Setting a contact’s next action makes a task on that contact; the contact still shows “Next: …” from its earliest open task. Existing next actions are copied in once, and the old column is dropped in a later deploy (the 0075 → 0076 pattern).

Alternative: keep “next action” separate and add tasks beside it — two lists of things to do, and the board would miss half the work.

2 · My day: built by rules from what is already there

Suggested

Each member’s My day, at the top of Home, lists in a fixed order: urgent new leads → today’s appointments by time → overdue tasks, oldest first → tasks due today → leads not yet contacted → cards waiting for their approval. Each line says why it is there. A morning email at a time each person chooses (off by default). The order is a rule anyone can read, not the AI’s opinion.

3 · Workload is counts, never a score

Suggested — strongly

Owners, admins and team leaders see per member: open tasks, overdue, leads not contacted, appointments this week, live listings, tenancies. Every number is a SQL count with a link to the rows behind it. No “busy score”, no productivity ranking, no colours that mark a person as slow — a count of things is information; a score of a person is a judgment the platform should not make. Agents see their own counts only.

4 · Overdue: the person first, their leader after N days

Suggested

An overdue task shows in My day and the bell for its owner. If it is still open after the days the agency sets (3 suggested), it appears on the team leader’s Overdue list — not the whole office’s. From there the leader can reschedule, reassign or close it with a reason.

5 · The AI helps with handovers and suggests moves; a person decides

Suggested

Reassigning (one task, or several at once) puts a card in front of the owner with a handover note the AI drafted from the contact’s timeline — labelled “Written by the AI”, holding no figures (a draft with one falls back to a template, as in the alert digest), editable, and sent only on approval. On the Workload page the assistant may suggest a move (“Ben has 2 overdue, Sarah has room this week”) from the counts it is handed; moving anything is the same card.

6 · Daily and weekly tasks for every member

Suggested

Owners set up repeating tasks — “Every Monday 9:00: call last weekend’s open-home visitors”, “Every day: clear your approvals” — for everyone, a team, or one person. The minute tick creates them on time; each lands in that person’s My day. A workflow step “Create a task” joins the catalogue, so workflows can make tasks too.

Not in this step

Later
  • Time tracking, timesheets, or hours per task.
  • Sharing a whole calendar with colleagues (appointments stay per person as now).
  • Sub-tasks and task dependencies; the assistant’s own plans stay as they are (ADR 0040).
