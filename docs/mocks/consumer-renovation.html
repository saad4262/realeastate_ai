Renovation AI — six decisions

The client's line: "analyse renovation opportunities and provide indicative planning and cost information". The hard part is the cost: there is no free, official source of renovation prices in Australia. Each decision has a recommendation; say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

  1. 1. Where the cost figures come from. Recommended: no dollar figure from us in version 1. Instead, for every piece of work: what drives the cost, what usually needs a licensed trade or a permit, and the buyer's own quotes compared side by side (decision 4). The page says plainly why there is no figure. Alternative A: license a cost guide (Cordell from CoreLogic, or Rawlinsons) — indicative ranges per item and per m², dated; needs the client's contract and budget, and only then figures appear. Alternative B: copy ranges from free commercial guides (hipages, Canstar…) — rejected: not ours to copy, not verifiable, often years old.
  2. 2. The AI turns words into a plan, from a fixed list of kinds of work.The buyer says what they want ("a new kitchen, open the wall to the dining room, a deck"). The AI picks matching items from ~25 kinds of work we define in code (kitchen, bathroom, removing a wall, extension, deck, re-roof, electrical upgrade…) and writes one line on each. Everything attached to a kind — the cost drivers, the trades, the permit notes — is written by us, not by the model. Recommended: yes — the model never invents a permit rule or a price.
  3. 3. Photos: rooms only, never condition.On a listing, the AI may look at the photos to say which rooms and features are shown (so the plan can start from "kitchen, 2 bathrooms, rear deck"). It never says what condition anything is in, never guesses age, defects, asbestos or what is behind a wall. Recommended: words first; photos as a button, rooms only. Photos cost more per call. Alternative: no photos in v1.
  4. 4. Your quotes, compared — the only figures on the page.The buyer types the quotes they get (trade, amount, what is included); the page adds them up and shows the difference between quotes for the same work. The arithmetic is code; the AI can point out what one quote includes that another does not — from the words the buyer typed — never the price. Recommended: yes, kept in this browser only (account saving comes with C4).
  5. 5. Permits and trades: per state, from official pages, links only.Which kinds of work usually need a building permit or approval, a licensed electrician or plumber, strata/owners-corporation approval, a heritage check; and the owner-builder rules. Verified per state like the checklists; a state not verified gets the general notes. Recommended: yes; "usually" — the council or a building surveyor decides.
  6. 6. Where it shows.A Renovation page (start from a listing or from scratch), a "Planning a renovation?" link on each listing for sale, and the guide's tool renovation_plan. All behind the Renovation AI switch (on by default — it answers). Recommended: all three.

What about the cost?

We do not show prices for renovation work: there is no official, current price list in Australia, and a figure we could not stand behind would be worse than none. Get at least three written quotes for each part, and compare them below.

Victoria — before you start Draft content · verified before build

  • Structural work, extensions and many decks need a building permit from a registered building surveyor.
  • Electrical, plumbing and gas work must be done by licensed trades, who give you a compliance certificate.
  • Doing it yourself as an owner-builder above a set value needs a certificate of consent.
  • A heritage overlay or an owners corporation may need its own approval — check VicPlan and the rules.

Victorian Building Authority · Consumer Affairs Victoria — links to the official pages.

If I bought 14 Example Street, how much would it cost to knock out the wall between the kitchen and dining room?
Renovation AI Removing a wall — Victoria
Drivers: load-bearing or not · services in the wall · making good · Needs: structural engineer and a building permit if load-bearing · asbestos test before 1990
I can't give you a price — there's no reliable price list for renovation work, so quotes are the only honest number. What decides the cost most is whether that wall is load-bearing: if it is, you'll need a structural engineer, a beam, and a building permit. Power or plumbing inside the wall adds to it, and if the house was built before 1990 it's worth an asbestos test first.
