Discovery & Opportunity — ten decisions

Two skills that point a buyer at homes they did not search for. Discovery AI suggests homes like one they are looking at, or like the profile they chose to keep, and includes a few homes that are not for sale. Opportunity AI finds homes for sale whose own records show something worth asking about: a price reduction, a long campaign, an asking price under what this site has seen sell. The risks are a model that ranks or judges homes nobody can check, approaches to owners who never chose to sell, and a "bargain" label that is really just a smaller house. Each decision has a recommendation. Say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

    Discovery AI

  1. 1. Built only from what you told us, in the open.There are two starting points. Similar homes on a listing uses that home's suburb, kind, bedrooms and price, uses no personal data, and works signed out. For you (signed in) uses your Personal Property AI profile (C4), only if you switched it on. If the profile is off, For you says so and offers the switch. Nothing is learnt from what you view or click in Discovery. Recommended: yes. Alternative: learn quietly from views and clicks. This goes against C4's rule that learning starts only when you switch it on.
  2. 2. Matched and ordered by code; every card says why.Postgres filters to homes for sale (live) in your channel and property kinds, priced at or under your budget (the top of a range counts). A home with no price shows "No price given". A card may differ on one thing and says which: a suburb not on your list but within 5 km (straight line) of one that is, or one more bedroom. It never goes over budget. Order: fewest differences, then nearest, then newest; up to 12. No model picks, scores or writes the reasons. Recommended: yes. Alternative: a model or embeddings (C9's provider) reranking from the ad text. It is harder to explain why a home appears, and it is metered.
  3. 3. Homes not for sale: only addresses this site sold, at least 12 months ago, in their own section.At most 3, labelled "Not for sale · sold on this site in Sep 2024" and never mixed with homes for sale. The first action is "Watch this address" (Property Monitor). "Make a private offer" comes second; it needs sign-in and goes to the agency that sold it (ADR 0012). A home bought less than 12 months ago never appears, nor does anything on the market or under offer. Today none qualify: all 5 sales on this site are from October 2026. Recommended: 12 months. Alternatives: 6 months; or no homes that are not for sale in C7.
  4. 4. "Not for me" hides a home from your suggestions.It is stored for you alone (migration 0087: discovery_hidden, owner-only in can(), classified PUBLIC_SITE_USER), can be undone from "Hidden homes", and is wiped by "Forget everything". It only hides; it does not teach the profile. Signed-out visitors cannot hide homes. Recommended: yes. Alternative: no hiding in C7, so no new table.

    5. Opportunity AI

  5. 5. Three signals, each from SQL, each with its date and numbers.
    • Price reduced: from the price-change records (C2's trigger). The low end went down, and Postgres computes the difference. "Contact agent" changing to a number is not a reduction. Price changes have been recorded only since 3 Nov 2026.
    • On the market a long time: 60 days or more since this campaign was published. The median for homes for sale today is 42 days. A relisted home counts from its new campaign and says so.
    • Asking below this site's median: compared only with sales on this site in the same suburb, of the same kind and the same number of bedrooms, and only with 10 or more such sales in 12 months (C5's rule). The number of sales is shown.
    For sale only; no rentals in C7. Recommended: yes. Alternative for the median: suburb and kind only. This shows more homes, but compares a 2-bedroom house with sales of 4-bedroom ones.
  6. 6. Facts, never a verdict.Each label is a fact: "Price reduced", "On the market 94 days", "Asking below this site's median". Each signal has a fixed paragraph we wrote about what it can and cannot mean, plus two questions to ask the agent. A test bans words like bargain, undervalued, under-priced, steal, motivated or desperate vendor, good deal from our text and the guide's prompt. No model-written paragraph on the page. Recommended: yes. Alternative: an AI paragraph on a button, checked like C5's (citations, figures, the verdict rule).
  7. 7. One public page, no badges, no email./opportunities filters by suburbs or a state, a price ceiling, bedrooms, kind and signal. "Match my profile" appears when Personal Property AI is on. The listing page is unchanged: C2's "Before you buy" already shows days on the market and price changes. No badges on search results. No email in C7; "tell me when a price drops" is an automation and belongs to C8. Recommended: yes. Alternative: a "Price reduced" badge on search result cards. It is visible to every buyer, and agencies may push back.
  8. 8. It says what it cannot show.Each signal shows how many homes in the area have it. An empty one says why: "price changes are recorded from 3 Nov 2026; none in Pakenham yet", or "the median needs 10 sales of 3-bedroom houses in 12 months; Pakenham has 4". Today: 6 homes for sale at 60+ days, 0 price changes, and no suburb with 10 sales. Recommended: yes.

    9. Both

  9. 9. The guide gets two tools, records only (prompt v16).discover_homes (from your profile when it is on, or from a listing id) and find_opportunities (area, ceiling, signals) return what the pages show, behind their own switches. The guide copies the facts and links to the page. It never calls a home a bargain and never guesses a value. Recommended: yes.
  10. 10. Switches and privacy.Both are "answers" skills, ON by default (ADR 0080). Similar homes and Opportunities are public; For you needs an account and the profile. No agency sees what a buyer was suggested, hid or opened (ADR 0011). Suggestions are worked out when the page opens and not stored; the only thing kept is what you hid. Recommended: yes.

Not in C7: rentals for either skill; "back on the market" (there is no status history yet); suggestions by email; a model ranking homes.

What this can mean, and what to ask

A lower asking price tells you the advertised figure moved. It doesn't tell you why, or what the seller will accept.
