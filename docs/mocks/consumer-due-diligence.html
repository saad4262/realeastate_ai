Due-Diligence AI — seven decisions

What to check before you buy, what our records show about the property, and what to ask the agent. Each decision has a recommendation; say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

  1. 1. Information, never legal advice.Every section ends: "General information, not legal advice. A conveyancer or solicitor should review the contract and the seller's documents before you sign." Recommended: yes, on every screen and under every guide answer.
  2. 2. The state checklist is written by us, not by the AI.Per state, from the official pages (Consumer Affairs Victoria, NSW Fair Trading, the Queensland Government's seller disclosure scheme…), each item with its source and the day it was checked — fixed in code like the duty rates, and re-checked with them every 1 July. A state we have not verified shows the general checklist and says so. Recommended: yes — the law is not something a model should improvise. Alternative: the AI writes each state's list (rejected: a wrong cooling-off period is worse than none).
  3. 3. "What our records show" is SQL, and the AI only summarises it.The facts: every sale at this address by any agency (ADR 0013), when this listing went live and how long it has been on the market, earlier listings that ended without a sale. Each line has a reference [R1]… The AI writes a short plain-English summary on a button, every sentence citing a reference, no figure of its own (ADR 0065's citation check rejects one that does). Labelled "Written by the property guide". Not saved; the facts are. Recommended: yes — facts always, the summary on a button.
  4. 4. Price changes are not recorded today.We store the current price only. Recommended: start recording from now — a small table written whenever an agency changes a listing's price (migration), shown as "Price changes since we began recording on [date]". Also unlocks ROADMAP 4.4's "price changes" trigger. Alternative: leave price changes out.
  5. 5. Maps: official links, never our own flood or zoning claim.Each state's planning map (VicPlan, NSW Planning Portal Spatial Viewer, PlanWA …) and the council's flood information, opened at the address where the site allows it. We show no overlay ourselves. Recommended: links only in C2; real overlay data waits for a data decision (C9, with Property Development AI).
  6. 6. Questions to ask the agent — the buyer sends them, not the AI.The AI drafts questions from the state checklist and the records ("It was listed in February 2024 and withdrawn in May — can you tell me why?"). The buyer ticks, edits, and the questions go into the listing's existing enquiry form, which the buyer sends. Sending by itself is Agent AI (C8, off until switched on). Recommended: yes.
  7. 7. Where it shows, and what is saved.A "Before you buy" section on each listing (replacing today's Victoria-only checklist link), the same on an address that is not for sale, and the guide's tool due_diligence. All behind the Due-Diligence AI switch (on by default — it answers). Checklist ticks are remembered in this browser only; saving them to the account comes with C4. Recommended: all three places; ticks in the browser for now.
