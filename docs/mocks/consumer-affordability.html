Affordability AI — six decisions

Each has a recommendation. Say "all recommended", or name the ones to change. The calculator and the guide are built only after this.

  1. 1. Estimates, never advice.Every money figure carries an "Estimate" tag and the line: "General information only, not financial or credit advice. Speak to a lender or a licensed broker before you decide." (NCCP — the platform gives no credit assistance.) Recommended: yes, on the page, on every listing panel and under every guide answer.
  2. 2. How "what could I borrow" is worked out.From what the buyer types: take-home pay, living expenses and other repayments. The surplus is tested at their interest rate plus 3% (APRA's serviceability buffer) over the loan term. Recommended: this — simple, nothing to keep up to date but the 3%. Alternative: gross income with tax tables and a living-expense floor (closer to a bank, but tax tables and HEM-style floors to maintain, and HEM itself is licensed).
  3. 3. Costs we do not estimate.Lenders mortgage insurance, lender fees, conveyancing, inspections, moving. The buyer types their own "Other costs"; under a 20% deposit the page says LMI may apply, with no figure. Recommended: yes — no number we cannot source.
  4. 4. A state rule we could not verify gets no figure.Today: the ACT's general rates, the Northern Territory, South Australia's first-home relief on new homes, Queensland's first-home vacant land concession. The page says so and links the official calculator. Inside a phase-out band whose formula the state does not publish (NSW $800k–$1m, VIC $600k–$750k) the figure is tagged Approximate. Recommended: yes. Rates re-checked every 1 July and after each state budget (dates are in the code).
  5. 5. Nothing is saved in version 1.The inputs live in the page address, so a scenario can be bookmarked or shared. Income and expenses are never stored or sent to the model. Saving scenarios comes with Personal Property AI (C4). Recommended: yes.
  6. 6. Where it shows.(a) An Affordability page; (b) a "Costs to buy this home" panel on every listing, filled with its state and price; (c) the guide's tool affordability_estimate: the model is handed the figures as a tool result and only explains them (non-negotiable #4). All three behind the Affordability AI switch in Skills (on by default — it only answers). Recommended: all three.

For a $750,000 established home in Queensland that you'll live in as your first home, the estimate is $10,925 in transfer duty — the first home concession takes $8,675 off the home concession rate. If it were a new home, a first home buyer in Queensland pays no transfer duty at any price.
