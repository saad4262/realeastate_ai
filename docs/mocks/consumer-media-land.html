Media & land — twelve decisions

The last three skills, and the first that depend on outside services. Immersive Property AI shows floorplans, video and tours. Property Development AI shows a property's zoning from the state, with what to ask before developing it. Visual Search AI finds homes that look like a description or a photo. The risks are a model reading a floorplan and inventing a size, a site that sounds like it is telling someone they can subdivide, and paying to search photos we don't have: there is one photo in the database today. The provider choices are on Providers. Each decision has a recommendation; say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

    Order

  1. 1. Build in this order: Immersive, then Development, then Visual Search.Immersive needs no new service: agents' floorplans are images, and their videos and tours are already links. Development uses free state data. Visual Search needs a paid embeddings provider and photos to search, and it is only useful once agencies' listings bring their photos in. Recommended: yes. Alternative: Visual Search first. It is the showiest of the three, but it can show nothing until there are photos.

    2. Immersive Property AI

  2. 2. Show what agents publish, with no video processing in C9.Floorplans are images, stored and served like photos (Supabase Storage, ADR 0009). Video and 3D tours are links agents already publish (YouTube, Vimeo, Matterport and other hosts on a fixed allowlist), shown as an embed that loads only when the buyer presses play, so no third-party cookie loads before that. The REAXML import learns floorplans, video links and tour links; the console gets "Add a floorplan / video link / tour link". Recommended: yes. Alternative: host video ourselves (Cloudflare Stream or Mux, on Providers). Worth it only if agencies want to upload raw video files.
  3. 3. The guide knows the rooms by name, never a size.A vision model reads the floorplan into room names only: kitchen, 3 bedrooms, study, deck. This is a strict schema, like C3's photo rooms. It never reads a dimension or an area, because a misread "4.2 × 3.6" is an invented number (non-negotiable 4). For sizes the guide says "they're on the floorplan" and links to it. Asked "will a king bed fit?", it points to the floorplan and the agent. Recommended: yes. Alternative: no AI on floorplans in C9, so the guide only links them.

    4. Property Development AI

  4. 4. Zoning from the state's own data, looked up by the property's map point.We use each state's official zone layer and show the zone name and code as the state publishes them, with the source and the date checked. A state with no usable source says "not verified" and links its official planning map (C2's pattern). The lookup is a network call, made before any transaction; the answer is kept per property for 30 days. Recommended: yes. Alternative: Geoscape's national Planning dataset. One schema and a paid licence; its Queensland data is from 2010 (on Providers).
  5. 5. Only data whose licence allows a commercial site.
    • Shown from day one: Victoria, South Australia (CC BY 4.0) and NSW (public Planning Portal service).
    • Shown once their terms are confirmed: ACT and Tasmania (licence not stated) and NT (CC BY 4.0, not yet verified at the source).
    • Link only: WA (CC BY-NC: commercial use needs the department's permission) and Queensland (no statewide layer; each council publishes its own).
    Recommended: yes.
  6. 6. Facts and questions, never advice.We show the zone, the land size from the ad, and a fixed list per state of questions for a town planner or the council: minimum lot size, overlays, permits, services, easements. Never "you can subdivide", "suits a dual occupancy", a lot count, a yield, or a value after development. No model writes on this panel; the guide copies it. Recommended: yes.
  7. 7. It appears on homes for sale and on addresses not for sale.Development questions matter most on land and older houses, often off the market. The panel shows on the listing and on /property/<id>, and only with a map point that came from the geocoder, never a guess. Recommended: yes.

    8. Visual Search AI

  8. 8. Provider: Voyage multimodal embeddings into pgvector.This is the provider the site's text search is already built for (behind its own switch), pgvector 0.8.2 is already in our Supabase, and the vectors stay in our own database. Cost is about $0.0006 per 1-megapixel photo, so 10,000 photos is about $6, with 150 billion pixels free. Every call is metered in ai_run, with a daily cap. See Providers. Recommended: yes. Alternatives: Cohere Embed v4; self-hosted CLIP or SigLIP; Pinecone for the vectors.
  9. 9. Search by words, or "more like this photo" on this site. No uploads in C9.A buyer can type "light-filled kitchen, timber floors" or press "More like this" on any listing photo. Uploading their own photo waits: it could be a person, someone's home, or an image they don't own, and we would be sending it to a provider. Recommended: yes. Alternative: allow uploads, embed them once, store nothing, faces not checked.
  10. 10. Looks are ranked within the buyer's filters, and never judged.Postgres applies the filters first (public listings, channel, area, price, bedrooms); the vectors only order what passes. Cards say "Looks similar" with the matching photo. They show no score, no claim about condition or quality, and nothing is written into the ad. A photo's vector exists only while its listing is public. Recommended: yes.
  11. 11. It says when there aren't enough photos.Below 500 photos on public listings the page says "Search by look is still filling up" with the count. Today it is 1. The skill stays "early" in Skills until then. The backfill runs only on your go, because it is paid. Recommended: yes; 500 photos.

    12. All three

  12. 12. "Answers" skills, ON by default, and the guide gets three tools.The tools are floorplan_rooms (names only), land_and_zoning (the panel's records) and homes_that_look_like (words or a photo id), all records only, prompt v18. No agency sees a buyer's searches. Each skill switches off on its own. Recommended: yes.

Not in C9: buyer photo uploads; self-hosted video; a model reading dimensions; development feasibility, yields or values; Queensland zoning by council (a later, per-council task).

Questions to ask before you develop

  1. What is the minimum lot size in this zone and schedule?
  2. Which overlays apply (heritage, flooding, bushfire, vegetation)?
  3. Is there a covenant or easement on the title?
  4. Is there capacity in the sewer, water and power connections?
  5. What permits would a second dwelling or a subdivision need?

Our questions, written for Victoria; the same for every property in the state.

Could I subdivide 14 Example Road?
land_and_zoning 14 Example Road, Officer → GRZ1 (Vicmap Planning, checked 4 Nov 2026) · 612 m² from the ad · Victoria's questions
It's in the General Residential Zone (Schedule 1), and the last ad gave the land as 612 m². The zone alone doesn't say whether it can be subdivided. That depends on the minimum lot size, any overlays, covenants and a planning permit. Worth asking a town planner or Cardinia council: what's the minimum lot size, which overlays apply, and what permits would a subdivision need?

Not allowed: "Yes, 612 m² is enough for two lots", "the main bedroom is about 4 × 3.5 m", "it'd be worth more subdivided".
