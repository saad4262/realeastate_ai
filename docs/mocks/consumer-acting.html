Acting for the buyer — twelve decisions

Three skills that do things, not just answer. Agent AI drafts a message to an agent, and the buyer reads it and sends it. Tasks runs research once or on a schedule and sends a report. Automation watches the homes a buyer follows and does what they asked: tell them, or tidy their shortlist. These are the first consumer skills that act on their own, so the main question is where acting stops. The recommended line is the buyer's own account: nothing ever reaches an agency unless the buyer presses Send on text they can see. Each decision has a recommendation; say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

    Agent AI

  1. 1. A draft, never a send.The guide writes the message into a card. The buyer can edit it, then presses Send to Ray White Pakenham. Nothing reaches an agency until they press Send on the exact text on screen. This is non-negotiable 7: anything model-written that reaches a third party needs a human. Recommended: yes.
  2. 2. A draft uses only what the buyer said, and the listing's facts.The model is handed the buyer's own words from this conversation and the listing's facts (address, price text, inspection times). It may not add a budget, finance, a timeline or a reason they did not state. It never makes an offer or names a price (offers go through the existing "Make an offer" form, ADR 0012), and never claims to be the buyer's agent or adviser. Core checks every draft: a figure that is neither in the buyer's words nor the listing's facts, an offer, or a claim on the buyer's behalf rejects the draft, and a plain template is used instead. Recommended: yes.
  3. 3. It goes through the doors that already exist.If there is no conversation yet, the message becomes an ordinary enquiry on the listing: a lead for the agency, assigned as today (ADR 0039). If the buyer already has a conversation with that agency about that home, it goes as a reply in it (ADR 0016). Name, email and phone come from the verified account, never from the model. At most 5 AI-drafted messages a day per buyer, so no agency gets a stream of them. Recommended: yes; 5 a day. Alternative: no daily limit beyond the enquiry form's own.
  4. 4. No "written by AI" line on the agency's copy.Once the buyer has read, edited and sent it, it is their message, as with a spell-checker. The buyer's own copy in Messages notes "Drafted with the guide". Recommended: no label to the agency. Alternative: a footer on the agency's copy, "Drafted with the site's guide".

    5. Tasks

  5. 5. A task is a saved schedule: the runner Smart Alerts already uses.The same search_schedule and schedule_run tables, with a kind (alert or task), the chosen sections, and a new "once" cadence (migration 0088). Times are in the buyer's own zone, and the cron tick is the same one with the same budget. Up to 10 tasks per buyer. Recommended: yes. Alternative: a separate task table and runner. That means a second clock to keep honest.
  6. 6. A report is made of sections from a fixed list, every figure from SQL.The list:
    • New homes matching a search.
    • What sold in the suburbs.
    • Price reductions (C7).
    • Long campaigns (C7).
    • The suburb's figures, with C5's 10-sale rule.
    • Open homes in the next 7 days.
    • What changed at the addresses they watch.
    The buyer picks up to four, on the page or by asking the guide, which fills a card they Accept. Nothing is scheduled without Accept, as with alerts today. Recommended: yes.
  7. 7. Each report is an email and a page.Each run is kept at /account/tasks/<id> and emailed with its own unsubscribe link. The short paragraph at the top follows the Smart Alerts rule: the model is handed no figures; every figure around it is rendered from SQL; any output containing a figure or a counting word, or failing C5's verdict rule, is replaced by a template; and it is labelled "Written by the property guide". A run with nothing new says so on the page and skips the email (the buyer can turn that off). Recommended: yes; skip empty emails.

    8. Automation

  8. 8. A fixed list of "when", about homes the buyer follows.A home on their shortlist or a watched address: price reduced, an open home added, under offer, sold or leased, no longer advertised, or the watched address listed. Plus: a new home matches a saved search. Detection is SQL on the existing tick (Property Monitor's checks, widened to the shortlist). No model anywhere in Automation. Recommended: yes.
  9. 9. A fixed list of "then", and it stops at the buyer's own account.Three actions:
    • Tell me (email).
    • Add it to my shortlist (for a new match).
    • Take it off my shortlist (when it sold or is no longer advertised).
    Never "enquire for me", "book an inspection" or "make an offer": anything that reaches an agency is Agent AI's draft-and-Send. Every action is logged on "What it did", with Undo. Recommended: yes.
  10. 10. When the email comes."Once a day at 8 am" (the default, the same email as Property Monitor), or "as it happens": checked hourly and held from 9 pm to 7 am. Each event fires once. Up to 20 automations per buyer, and each email has an unsubscribe link for that automation. Recommended: yes.

    11. All three

  11. 11. All three are "acts": off until switched on, and they need an account.Switching one off stops it at once (the cron skips it, as with Smart Alerts) and keeps what was set up, so switching it back on resumes. The platform pause covers all three. No agency sees a buyer's tasks or automations. The agency sees an Agent AI message only as the ordinary enquiry or reply it is. Recommended: yes.
  12. 12. The guide proposes, the buyer decides.Three tools, draft_message, draft_task and draft_automation, each return a card and never act (prompt v17). The guide may say "I've drafted…", never "I've sent…" or "it's running". Recommended: yes.

Not in C8: SMS or push notifications (email only); automations about homes the buyer does not follow; a task that writes to anyone but the buyer; booking an inspection time (agents do not publish bookable slots).
