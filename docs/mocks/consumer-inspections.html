Inspections — nine decisions

Two skills for the day a buyer actually walks through homes. Inspection AI turns the open-home times agents already publish into a plan for the day, with a calendar file and a route. Inspection Assistant is the buyer's own notebook at each home: a checklist, notes and photos, private to them, with a summary written on request. The hard parts are travel time (we have no honest number for it) and privacy (notes and photos of someone's home). Each decision has a recommendation; say "all recommended" or name the ones to change.

  1. 1. The times are the agents'; the planner orders and checks, it never invents.A plan is built only from open-home times in our inspection table (the ones on each listing page). The planner picks one time for each home, puts them in order, and flags any stop it cannot reach. It never moves a time and never drops a home silently: a home that does not fit stays on the plan as "Not this time", with its other times offered. When an agent changes or cancels a time, the plan shows it the next time it is opened. Recommended: yes.
  2. 2. Travel: a straight-line distance and a gap you choose — no driving time.Between stops we show the straight-line distance from the database (PostGIS), labelled "straight line". The buyer sets the gap they need between homes (default 20 minutes) and the time they want inside each (default 10 minutes). A stop is "Tight" when, after that, less than 10 minutes of its open home would be left, and "Can't make it" when none would. We do not estimate driving time: that needs a paid routing service, and a guessed time is an invented number. Recommended: straight line + your own gap. Alternative: a routing API (Google Routes) for real driving times — metered per plan, and another outside service.
  3. 3. Calendar and map without connecting an account."Add to calendar" downloads one .ics file with an event per stop (address, times, a link to the listing), built by the iCalendar helper the console's meeting emails already use. "Open the route" opens Google Maps with the stops in order (a plain link, no API). We never connect to the buyer's calendar. Recommended: yes.
  4. 4. The planner is public; the plan lives in the link.No account needed (Inspection AI is an "answers" skill, ON by default). The plan is /inspections?s=… (the chosen times), so it can be shared with a partner and reopened. Ways in: "Add to my day" on each time on a listing page, "Plan inspections" on the shortlist, and the guide. Up to 8 homes in one day. Recommended: yes; up to 8 homes. Alternative: saved plans on the account — more to store, little gained over a link.
  5. 5. The guide plans, but never reads your notes.The guide gets one tool, plan_inspections (listing ids and a day → the ordered stops, distances and flags, all from SQL and PostGIS), and links to the plan page. Inspection notes are never given to the guide or to any chat in C6. Recommended: yes.
  6. 6. Inspection notes are yours alone.Inspection Assistant needs an account. Notes, ticks and photos are owner-only in can() — no agency, agent or platform admin can read them, or even see that they exist. They are not shared with the listing's agency and never feed leads, the customer profile or the Personal Property AI. Recommended: yes — the same wall as the shortlist and watch (C4).
  7. 7. A fixed checklist, in our own words.About 15 things to look at, grouped (outside, inside, services, the street), with a house version and an apartment version (the apartment one adds strata items, linked to C2's due-diligence checklist for the state). Each item: "Looks fine", "Issue", or "Didn't check", plus an optional note. The AI does not write the checklist. Recommended: yes.
  8. 8. Photos: private, location removed, never looked at by the AI.Up to 20 photos per home, 10 MB each, in a private Supabase Storage bucket (ADR 0009), shown through short-lived signed links. The phone's location (GPS) is removed from each photo before upload. The AI never sees the photos — as in C3, no one describes a home's condition from pictures. Deleting a home's notes deletes its photos from storage; "Delete all my inspection notes" deletes everything. Recommended: yes. Alternative: no photos in v1 (notes and ticks only) — no storage, less useful on the day.
  9. 9. The summary: from your own notes only, on a button, labelled."Summarise my day" (or one home) gives the model only the buyer's own ticks and notes, as cited records. Every line cites them; a line with a figure not in the buyer's own words is dropped (the C2 check); no verdict ("buy this one", "the best of the four"). It goes only to the buyer, carries "Written by the property guide" (non-negotiable 7's scope), is rate-limited, and is not stored — it is written again on request. Recommended: yes.
